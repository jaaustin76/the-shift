THE SHIFT

PROTOTYPE v0.1 — CORE LOOP TEST

You run a sort aisle. Packages come off the trailer, ride the belt, and drop into four chutes. Each chute loads a trailer at a dock.

You have three people and four lanes. You are always one short, and a chute with nobody on it loads nothing at all — it just fills. Tap a lane to move crew onto it, up to two each.

A chute holds 8. When it fills, the next package for that chute stops dead on the belt — and nothing behind it can get past. One neglected lane will strangle the entire building. The banner will tell you which chute is doing it.

Later you can buy a recirculation line so blocked packages loop round and try again instead of stopping everything. Until then, keep every chute breathing.

Crew have to walk. Moving someone across the floor takes about 6 seconds — 2 between neighbouring chutes — and they load nothing on the way. A dashed circle means they're still walking.

So chasing is a trap. A chute overflows in about 10 seconds and the walk eats most of that. Moving someone every time a chute looks bad means they spend the shift walking and load almost nothing. Pick a position, commit to it, and only move when the gain clearly beats the walk.

One shift is 3 minutes. Ship as many packages as you can.

THE LAB

CHANGE THE WAREHOUSE, NOT THE PLAYER

Automated testing found that how you play changes throughput by about 2%, while what the warehouse is made of changes it by 79%. Try it: play a shift, then change one structural slider and play again. The difference will dwarf anything you do with your thumbs.
